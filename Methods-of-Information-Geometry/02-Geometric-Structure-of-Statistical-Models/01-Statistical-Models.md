# Statistical Models

## TL;DR

- A statistical model is a family $S = \{p_\xi = p(x; \xi) \mid \xi \in \Xi\}$ of probability distributions on $\mathcal{X}$ indexed injectively by an open $\Xi \subset \mathbb{R}^n$.
- Regularity assumptions: $\xi \mapsto p(x; \xi)$ is $C^\infty$, differentiation and integration can be swapped (so $\int\partial_ip\,dx = \partial_i1 = 0$), and the support $\{x \mid p(x; \xi) > 0\}$ does not depend on $\xi$.
- With constant support, $S$ is a subset of $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}) = \{p > 0, \int p = 1\}$, and $\xi$ is a global coordinate system: $S$ is a statistical manifold, and "the point $\xi$" means the distribution $p_\xi$.
- Smooth reparametrisations $\rho = \psi(\xi)$ describe the same manifold; geometric statements must not depend on the parametrisation.
- Standard examples: normal ($n = 2$), multivariate normal ($n = k + k(k+1)/2$), Poisson ($n = 1$) and the full family $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$ on a finite set ($n = \lvert\mathcal{X}\rvert - 1$).

## Learning objectives

- Define a parametric statistical model and the role of the true distribution $p^*$.
- State the regularity assumptions the book places on models and say why each is needed.
- Treat a statistical model as a manifold with $\xi$ as coordinates.
- Write down the four standard examples and their dimensions.

## Core concepts

### Distributions and densities

On a discrete (finite or countable) $\mathcal{X}$ a probability distribution is $p : \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ with

$$
p(x) \ge 0, \qquad \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}}p(x) = 1, \tag{2.1}
$$

and on $\mathcal{X} = \mathbb{R}^n$ a density with

$$
p(x) \ge 0, \qquad \int p(x)\,dx = 1. \tag{2.2}
$$

More generally $p = dP/d\nu$ is a Radon-Nikodym derivative with respect to a $\sigma$-finite measure $\nu$ on $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{B})$.
The book writes everything with the integral form; for discrete $\mathcal{X}$ replace $\int\cdots dx$ by $\sum_x$.

### Statistical models

A family of distributions parametrised by $n$ real variables,

$$
S = \{p_\xi = p(x; \xi) \mid \xi = [\xi^1, \dots, \xi^n] \in \Xi\}, \tag{2.3}
$$

with $\Xi \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\xi \mapsto p_\xi$ injective, is an $n$-dimensional statistical model (parametric model, or model).
Statistics starts from data $x_1, \dots, x_N$ assumed to be generated by an unknown true (underlying) distribution $p^*$; prior knowledge fixes the "shape" of $p^*$ up to a few free parameters, and $S$ is the set of candidates.

### Regularity assumptions

The book assumes:

1. $\Xi$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and, for each $x$, $\xi \mapsto p(x; \xi)$ is $C^\infty$, so $\partial_ip(x; \xi)$ and $\partial_i\partial_jp(x; \xi)$ exist ($\partial_i = \partial/\partial \xi^i$);
2. integration and differentiation can be interchanged, for example

$$
\int\partial_ip(x; \xi)\,dx = \partial_i\int p(x; \xi)\,dx = \partial_i1 = 0; \tag{2.4}
$$

3. the support $\text{supp}(p) = \{x \mid p(x) > 0\}$ does not depend on $\xi$; redefining $\mathcal{X}$ as this common support, $p(x; \xi) > 0$ for all $\xi$ and $x$, so $S$ is a subset of

$$
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}) = \left\{p : \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R} \;\middle|\; p(x) > 0 \;(\forall x \in \mathcal{X}), \int p(x)\,dx = 1\right\}. \tag{2.5}
$$

Models whose support moves with $\xi$ (for example the uniform $U(0, \xi)$) cause serious difficulties and are excluded.

### Standard examples

| Example | $\mathcal{X}$ | $n$ | Parameters | $p(x; \xi)$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2.1 Normal | $\mathbb{R}$ | 2 | $\xi = [\mu, \sigma]$, $\sigma > 0$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp\{-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\}$ |
| 2.2 Multivariate normal | $\mathbb{R}^k$ | $k + \frac{k(k+1)}{2}$ | $\xi = [\mu, \Sigma]$, $\Sigma$ positive definite | $(2\pi)^{-k/2}(\det\Sigma)^{-1/2}\exp\{-\frac12(x - \mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x - \mu)\}$ |
| 2.3 Poisson | $\{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ | 1 | $\xi > 0$ | $e^{-\xi}\xi^x/x!$ |
| 2.4 $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$, finite $\mathcal{X}$ | $\{x_0, \dots, x_n\}$ | $n$ | $\xi^i > 0$, $\sum_{i=1}^n\xi^i < 1$ | $p(x_i; \xi) = \xi^i$ for $i \ge 1$, $p(x_0; \xi) = 1 - \sum_i\xi^i$ |

### Statistical manifolds

The map $\varphi(p_\xi) = \xi$ is a coordinate system on $S$.
If $\psi : \Xi \to \psi(\Xi)$ is a $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism onto an open set, $\rho = \psi(\xi)$ gives the same family as $S = \{p_{\psi^{-1}(\rho)} \mid \rho \in \psi(\Xi)\}$.
Treating all smoothly related parametrisations as equivalent makes $S$ a $C^\infty$ manifold, a statistical manifold, with $\xi$ a global coordinate system.
From now on the book conflates $p_\xi$ with its coordinate $\xi$ ("the point $\xi$", "the tangent space $T_\xi(S)$").

## Worked examples

### Counting dimensions

The multivariate normal on $\mathbb{R}^3$ has $3$ mean parameters and $3 \cdot 4/2 = 6$ free covariance entries, so $n = 9$; on $\mathbb{R}^{10}$ it has $10 + 55 = 65$.
$\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$ with $\lvert\mathcal{X}\rvert = 3$ is 2-dimensional: the point $\xi = (0.2, 0.3)$ is the distribution $p = (0.5, 0.2, 0.3)$ on $(x_0, x_1, x_2)$, an interior point of the open triangle.

### The score has mean zero

For the normal family $\ell = -\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} - \log\sigma - \frac12\log 2\pi$, so

$$
\partial_\mu\ell = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma^2}, \qquad \partial_\sigma\ell = \frac{(x - \mu)^2}{\sigma^3} - \frac1\sigma,
$$

and both have expectation 0, as (2.4) requires once rewritten as $E_\xi[\partial_i\ell] = 0$ (section 2.2).

### Why moving supports are excluded

For $U(0, \xi)$, $p(x; \xi) = 1/\xi$ on $(0, \xi)$, so on the support $\partial_\xi\log p = -1/\xi$ and $E_\xi[\partial_\xi\log p] = -1/\xi \neq 0$.
(2.4) fails because $\int_0^\xi p\,dx$ has a boundary term when differentiated: the derivative moves the endpoint of integration.
The Fisher-information machinery breaks: estimation for this model converges at rate $1/N$ instead of $1/\sqrt N$, and the Cramer-Rao bound does not apply.

### Injectivity

The mixture $\frac12N(a, 1) + \frac12N(b, 1)$ parametrised by $(a, b)$ is not a statistical model in the book's sense, because $(a, b)$ and $(b, a)$ give the same distribution; restricting to $a < b$ restores injectivity on an open set.

## Pitfalls

- Confusing the model with the true distribution; $p^*$ need not lie in $S$, and much of chapter 3 (projections, divergences) is about the case where it does not.
- Allowing boundary points, such as $\sigma = 0$ or a face of the simplex where some $p(x) = 0$; these violate positivity or openness, and $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$ is the open simplex.
- Assuming a parametrisation is geometric; only properties invariant under the diffeomorphisms $\psi$ are properties of $S$.
- Forgetting that the regularity conditions are assumptions; mixture models at singular points and change-point models violate them, and those need separate theory.

## Review questions

> [!question]- ig-sm-definition | What is an $n$-dimensional statistical model in the book?
> A family $S = \{p(x; \xi) \mid \xi \in \Xi\}$ of distributions on $\mathcal{X}$ with $\Xi \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ open and $\xi \mapsto p_\xi$ injective, smooth in $\xi$, with constant support and differentiation under the integral allowed.

> [!question]- ig-sm-regularity | Why does the book assume the support of $p(x; \xi)$ does not depend on $\xi$?
> So that $\log p$ is defined everywhere and differentiating $\int p\,dx = 1$ in $\xi$ gives $\int\partial_ip\,dx = 0$ without boundary terms. Moving supports (like $U(0, \xi)$) break the score identity and the Fisher-information theory.

> [!question]- ig-sm-score-zero | What does equation (2.4) say, and what does it imply for the score?
> $\int\partial_ip(x; \xi)\,dx = \partial_i\int p\,dx = 0$. Dividing by $p$ inside the integral gives $E_\xi[\partial_i\log p] = 0$: the score has mean zero.

> [!question]- ig-sm-dims | Give the dimension of the normal, the multivariate normal on $\mathbb{R}^k$, the Poisson, and $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$ for $\lvert\mathcal{X}\rvert = n + 1$.
> 2, $k + k(k+1)/2$, 1 and $n$.

> [!question]- ig-sm-manifold | In what sense is a statistical model a manifold?
> $\xi$ is a global coordinate system (injective onto an open set), and any $C^\infty$ diffeomorphic reparametrisation $\rho = \psi(\xi)$ is an equally valid one. The manifold is the set of distributions, independent of the labelling.

> [!question]- ig-sm-uniform | Compute $E_\xi[\partial_\xi\log p]$ for $U(0, \xi)$ and explain the result.
> $-1/\xi$, not 0. The support depends on $\xi$, so differentiating $\int_0^\xi p\,dx = 1$ produces a boundary term and (2.4) fails.

> [!question]- ig-sm-simplex-point | On $\mathcal{X} = \{x_0, x_1, x_2\}$ with the coordinates of Example 2.4, which distribution is $\xi = (0.2, 0.3)$?
> $p(x_1) = 0.2$, $p(x_2) = 0.3$, $p(x_0) = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5$.
